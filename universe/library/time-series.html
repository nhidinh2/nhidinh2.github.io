Time series

When observations arrive in order, their dependence becomes a feature rather than a nuisance. Time-series models describe that dependence explicitly so we can estimate, diagnose, and forecast.

Stationarity

A time series $\{X_t\}$ is stationary when the mean $\mu_t$ is constant and the autocovariance $\gamma(s, t)$ depends on $s, t$ only through $|s - t|$. Stationarity is what lets us estimate covariances from a single realization.

Trend, differencing, and random walks

Most raw series are not stationary—they trend or drift. The random walk $X_t = X_{t-1} + W_t$ is the canonical example: its variance $\operatorname{Var}(X_t) = t\,\sigma_w^2$ grows without bound and its ACF decays only linearly, the classic signature of a unit root (a root of $\phi(B)$ on the unit circle). Two cures:

  • Differencing: $\nabla X_t = (1 - B) X_t = X_t - X_{t-1}$ removes a linear trend and turns a random walk into white noise; $\nabla^d$ removes a degree-$d$ polynomial trend. Seasonal differencing $\nabla_s X_t = (1 - B^s) X_t$ removes a repeating period-$s$ pattern.
  • Detrending by regression: fit $X_t = \beta_0 + \beta_1 t + Y_t$ and model the stationary residual $Y_t$. Prefer this when the trend is genuinely deterministic; prefer differencing when it is stochastic.

Formal check: the augmented Dickey–Fuller test has $H_0$ = “unit root present” (needs differencing) against stationarity. Over-differencing is a real cost—it injects an MA unit root and inflates variance—so difference only until the ACF stops decaying slowly.

AR, MA, and ARMA

AR(p): $$X_t = \phi_1 X_{t-1} + \phi_2 X_{t-2} + \cdots + \phi_p X_{t-p} + W_t, \qquad W_t \sim \operatorname{wn}(0, \sigma_w^2).$$ With the backshift operator this becomes $\phi(B)\, X_t = W_t$ where $\phi(B) = 1 - \phi_1 B - \cdots - \phi_p B^p$. Causality requires all roots of $\phi(B)$ to lie outside the unit circle, giving $$X_t = \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \psi_j W_{t-j}, \qquad \psi_0 = 1,\ \psi_j = \phi_1 \psi_{j-1} + \cdots + \phi_p \psi_{j-p}.$$ For AR(1): $$\gamma(h) = \frac{\phi^{\,h} \sigma_w^2}{1 - \phi^2}, \qquad \rho(h) = \phi^{\,h}.$$

MA(q): $$X_t = W_t + \theta_1 W_{t-1} + \cdots + \theta_q W_{t-q} = \theta(B)\, W_t.$$ Invertibility (all roots of $\theta(B)$ outside the unit circle) gives $W_t = \pi(B)\, X_t$ with $$\pi_0 = 1, \qquad \pi_j = -\bigl[\theta_1 \pi_{j-1} + \cdots + \theta_q \pi_{j-q}\bigr].$$

ARMA(p, q): $\phi(B)\, X_t = \theta(B)\, W_t$, with the parameter-redundancy rule that $\phi(B)$ and $\theta(B)$ share no common factors. The $\psi$ and $\pi$ weights satisfy $$\psi_j = \theta_j + \phi_1 \psi_{j-1} + \cdots + \phi_p \psi_{j-p},$$ $$\pi_j = -\phi_j - \bigl[\theta_1 \pi_{j-1} + \cdots + \theta_q \pi_{j-q}\bigr].$$

ACF and PACF

For a causal ARMA(p, q), $$\gamma(h) = \sum_{j=1}^{p} \phi_j\, \gamma(h - j) \quad \text{for } h > q,$$ with the MA cross term $\sigma_w^2 \sum_{j=h}^{q} \theta_j \psi_{j-h}$ added when $0 \le h \le q$. If $X_t = \sum_{j \ge 0} \psi_j W_{t-j}$ then $$\gamma(h) = \sigma_w^2 \sum_{j=0}^{\infty} \psi_j\, \psi_{j+h}, \qquad \rho(h) = \frac{\sum_{j \ge 0} \psi_j \psi_{j+h}}{\sum_{j \ge 0} \psi_j^2}.$$

The partial autocorrelation $\phi_{hh}$ is the correlation between $X_{t+h}$ and $X_t$ after removing the linear effect of the intermediate lags. For AR(p) the PACF cuts off after lag $p$; for MA(q) the ACF cuts off after lag $q$.

  • AR(p): ACF tails off, PACF cuts off at $p$
  • MA(q): ACF cuts off at $q$, PACF tails off
  • ARMA(p, q): both tail off
  • Large-sample bands: $\hat{\rho}(h) \sim N(0, 1/n)$ beyond the cutoff

Estimation

Method of moments (Yule–Walker for AR(p)): $$\hat{R}_p\, \hat{\phi} = \hat{\rho}_p, \qquad \hat{\phi} = \hat{R}_p^{-1} \hat{\rho}_p, \qquad \hat{\sigma}_w^2 = \hat{\gamma}(0)\bigl[\, 1 - \hat{\rho}_p^{\!\top} \hat{\phi}\, \bigr].$$ For MA(1), $\hat{\rho}(1) = \hat{\theta} / (1 + \hat{\theta}^2)$ has a real root only when $|\hat{\rho}(1)| \lt 1/2$.

Conditional least squares / MLE: minimize $S_c = \sum_{t=p+1}^{n} W_t(\phi)^2$. For AR(p) the CLS estimator reduces to OLS, $$\hat{\phi} = (X'X)^{-1} X'y, \qquad \hat{\sigma}^2 = \frac{1}{n - p}\sum_{t=p+1}^{n} W_t(\hat{\phi})^2.$$ For MA and ARMA the innovations are nonlinear in the parameters, so Gauss–Newton is used: $$\hat{\theta}_{\ell+1} = \hat{\theta}_{\ell} + \Bigl[\sum_t \dot{W}_t \dot{W}_t^{\!\top}\Bigr]^{-1} \Bigl[\sum_t \dot{W}_t\, W_t\Bigr], \qquad \dot{W}_t = -\frac{\partial W_t}{\partial \theta}.$$

ARIMA and SARIMA

ARIMA(p, d, q): $$\phi(B)\,(1 - B)^d X_t = \alpha + \theta(B)\, W_t, \qquad \alpha = \mu\,(1 - \phi_1 - \cdots - \phi_p).$$ Differencing $d$ times turns a trending series into a stationary one.

SARIMA$(p, d, q) \times (P, D, Q)_s$: $$\Phi(B^s)\, \phi(B)\, (1 - B)^d (1 - B^s)^D X_t = \mu + \Theta(B^s)\, \theta(B)\, W_t,$$ adding a seasonal layer at period $s$.

  • Increase $d$ until the ACF stops decaying slowly; increase $D$ until seasonal spikes disappear.
  • Read non-seasonal $(p, q)$ from lags $1, \ldots, s-1$; seasonal $(P, Q)$ from lags $s, 2s, \ldots$
  • Model selection: lowest AIC/BIC, no significant residual-ACF spikes, Ljung–Box $p > 0.05$, roughly normal Q–Q plot.

Forecasting

The minimum-MSE forecast is the conditional expectation $$\hat{X}_{n+m \mid n} = E[X_{n+m} \mid X_n, X_{n-1}, \ldots].$$ For AR(1), $$\hat{X}_{n+m \mid n} = \phi^{m} X_n, \qquad P^{\,n}_{n+m} = \bigl(1 + \phi^2 + \cdots + \phi^{\,2(m-1)}\bigr)\sigma_w^2.$$

The best linear predictor on the last $p$ observations solves the Toeplitz system $\Gamma_p\, \phi^{(m)} = \gamma_m$, giving $$\hat{X}_{n+m \mid n} = \sum_{j=1}^{p} \phi^{(m)}_{nj}\, X_{n+1-j}, \qquad \operatorname{MSPE}(m) = \gamma(0) - \phi^{(m)\top} \gamma_m.$$ For invertible models the infinite-past forecast $\tilde{X}_{n+1} = -\sum_{j \ge 1} \pi_j X_{n+1-j}$ has error $W_{n+1}$ and MSPE $\sigma_w^2$.

Worked identification example

Box–Jenkins modeling is a loop: identify orders from the correlograms, estimate parameters, diagnose residuals, then forecast. Suppose a differenced, mean-centered series shows this pattern:

  • Sample ACF: a single spike at lag 1 ($\hat\rho(1) \approx -0.4$), everything else inside the $\pm 2/\sqrt{n}$ bands.
  • Sample PACF: decaying, alternating spikes at lags 1, 2, 3, …

ACF cutting off at lag 1 while the PACF tails off is the MA(1) signature, so we fit $X_t = W_t + \theta_1 W_{t-1}$. The moment estimate solves $\hat\rho(1) = \hat\theta_1/(1 + \hat\theta_1^2)$; since $|\hat\rho(1)| = 0.4 \lt 1/2$ a real invertible root exists. Had we started from an undifferenced series, the slowly decaying ACF would have told us to difference first—so the full call here is ARIMA(0, 1, 1), which is equivalent to simple exponential smoothing below.

Diagnostics and model checking

A good model leaves residuals that look like white noise. After fitting, examine $\hat W_t = X_t - \hat X_{t \mid t-1}$:

  • Residual ACF: no spikes should escape the $\pm 2/\sqrt{n}$ bands; a surviving spike at lag $s$ signals missed seasonality.
  • Ljung–Box test: the portmanteau statistic $$Q = n(n+2) \sum_{h=1}^{H} \frac{\hat\rho_{\hat W}(h)^2}{n - h} \;\sim\; \chi^2_{H - p - q}$$ tests whether the first $H$ residual autocorrelations are jointly zero. Want a large $p$-value ($p \gt 0.05$): failing to reject means the residuals are adequately white.
  • Normality: a roughly straight residual Q–Q plot supports the Gaussian forecast intervals.
  • Selection: among adequate models, prefer the lowest AIC/BIC—BIC penalizes parameters more heavily and favors parsimony.

Exponential smoothing

When a full ARIMA fit is overkill, exponential smoothing gives robust forecasts by weighting recent observations more heavily. Simple exponential smoothing updates a level: $$\hat X_{t+1} = \alpha X_t + (1 - \alpha)\hat X_t = \alpha \sum_{j \ge 0}(1 - \alpha)^j X_{t-j}, \qquad 0 \lt \alpha \lt 1,$$ which is exactly the optimal forecast of an ARIMA(0, 1, 1) model. Two extensions handle richer structure:

  • Holt's linear trend: smooth a level and a slope separately, so forecasts extrapolate along a trend rather than flatlining.
  • Holt–Winters: add a third recursion for a seasonal component (additive or multiplicative), the standard quick tool for seasonal series.
